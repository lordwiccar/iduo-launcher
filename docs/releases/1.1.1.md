# iDuo Launcher 1.1.1

Third release on Google Play (version code 4). It gives the cover screen its own Home, adds an iPhone-like app library, app menus and icon packs, and brings a frosted, blurred look.

## Highlights

- **Separate cover Home**: mirror the inner screen or give the cover its own pages, with up to five columns when its dock slides in from the edge or hides.
- **Library and On Home**: two new All apps views. Library sorts apps into folders by purpose; On Home places every app on Home pages.
- **App menu** on a long-pressed app with its shortcuts, Remove, Uninstall, Icon and App info.
- **Icon packs** from Google Play, for all apps or a single one.
- **Dock folders** and recently opened apps in the dock's empty places.
- **Your photo, your crop**: move and zoom a photo wallpaper separately for each screen, already in its preview.
- **Blur and frost**: a blurred wallpaper behind All apps, the news page and folders, and a frosted dock, including on Samsung with wallpapers chosen in Samsung's settings.
- A redesigned status ring, at the top or above the dock on the cover screen.
- A notification after each update from Google Play that opens the changelog.

## Install

Install or update from Google Play, or download `iDuoLauncher-1.1.1-release.apk` from this release. It updates 1.1.0 installed from the same source; the Play version and the GitHub APK are signed differently and do not update each other.

## Privacy

No account, ads, analytics or tracking. Two new optional permissions: notifications, for the update notice, and the request to delete packages, which only opens Android's own uninstall confirmation. See the [privacy policy](../privacy-policy.md).

## Known limits

- The optional accessibility service is needed to open notifications and Quick Settings with a swipe down and to lock the screen with a double tap.
- A photo's crop applies to iDuo's Home; Android crops the lock screen itself. Wallpapers chosen in Android's or Samsung's picker are adjusted there.
- On Samsung the blur uses Samsung's own window blur; elsewhere Android's blur, where the device allows it.
